Conventional analysis of service systems typically starts with a specified model that describes how the system operates and generates data. In queueing systems, for example, the model specifies arrival and service processes, a service discipline, and a number of servers. I study the inverse problem: learning operational dynamics from data. I develop methods that use the ordering and dependencies of operational events, such as customer arrivals and service completions, to guide machine learning. These methods enable the construction of data-driven simulators for operational decision-making and support counterfactual policy evaluation, capacity allocation, and online pricing. I am also extending these ideas to inventory systems.
